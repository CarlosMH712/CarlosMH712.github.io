Learning objectives

What you should be able to do

  • Apply conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across a normal shock.
  • Calculate \(M_2\), pressure, density, temperature, and total-pressure ratios from \(M_1\).
  • Explain why a physical stationary normal shock requires \(M_1>1\).
  • Distinguish static-pressure rise from total-pressure loss.
  • Interpret subsonic and supersonic Pitot measurements correctly.

Physical behavior

An abrupt irreversible compression

A normal shock is an extremely thin region in which a supersonic flow becomes subsonic. Static pressure, density, temperature, and entropy increase; velocity and Mach number decrease.

Conserved: mass flow, total enthalpy, and total temperature for an adiabatic shock with no work.
Not conserved: entropy increases and total pressure decreases.

Control volume

Three conservation equations

For steady, one-dimensional, adiabatic flow through an equal-area control volume:

Mass

\[ \boxed{\rho_1u_1=\rho_2u_2} \]

Momentum

\[ \boxed{p_1+\rho_1u_1^2=p_2+\rho_2u_2^2} \]

Energy

\[ \boxed{h_1+\frac{u_1^2}{2}=h_2+\frac{u_2^2}{2}} \]
Why the shock is irreversible even though the outer states are treated as inviscid

The uniform regions upstream and downstream may be modeled as inviscid, but the internal shock structure contains very large velocity and temperature gradients. Viscosity and heat conduction generate entropy inside that microscopic layer. The jump model relates the two equilibrium states without resolving the internal thickness.

Closed-form solution

Normal-shock relations

For a calorically perfect gas, all downstream ratios depend only on \(M_1\) and \(\gamma\).

Downstream Mach

\[ \boxed{ M_2^2= \frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2} {\gamma M_1^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}} } \]

Pressure ratio

\[ \boxed{ \frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2-1) } \]

Density ratio

\[ \boxed{ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2} {2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2} } \]

Temperature ratio

\[ \boxed{ \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{p_2/p_1}{\rho_2/\rho_1} } \]
For air with \(\gamma=1.4\), the maximum density ratio in the calorically perfect strong-shock limit is \((\gamma+1)/(\gamma-1)=6\).

Second law

Entropy generation and total-pressure loss

Conservation equations admit an algebraic expansion-shock branch, but the second law rejects it. The physically admissible stationary shock has \(M_1>1\), \(M_2<1\), and \(s_2>s_1\).

\[ \boxed{ \frac{s_2-s_1}{R} = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) } \]

Total-pressure ratio

\[ \boxed{ \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_{0,1}} = \left[ \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2} {2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2} \right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} \left[ \frac{\gamma+1} {2\gamma M_1^2-(\gamma-1)} \right]^{1/(\gamma-1)} } \]
Static pressure increases across a shock, but the recoverable total pressure decreases. These are different quantities and should never be used interchangeably.

Instrumentation

Subsonic and supersonic Pitot measurements

Subsonic flow

The flow decelerates approximately isentropically to the probe opening, so the measured pressure is the upstream total pressure:

\[ p_{\mathrm{Pitot}}=p_{0,1}, \qquad M_1^2= \frac{2}{\gamma-1} \left[ \left(\frac{p_{0,1}}{p_1}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1 \right]. \]

Supersonic flow

A bow shock forms ahead of the probe. Along the centerline, the flow passes through a locally normal shock and then decelerates isentropically from region 2 to the stagnation point.

Rayleigh Pitot formula

\[ \boxed{ \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_1} = \left[ \frac{(\gamma+1)^2M_1^2} {4\gamma M_1^2-2(\gamma-1)} \right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} \left[ \frac{1-\gamma+2\gamma M_1^2} {\gamma+1} \right] } \]

For air, the sonic dividing pressure ratio is

\[ \left.\frac{p_{\mathrm{Pitot}}}{p_1}\right|_{M=1}=1.8929. \]

Worked example

Normal shock at \(M_1=2\)

Problem

Air at \(M_1=2.00\), \(p_1=50.0\ \text{kPa}\), and \(T_1=220\ \text{K}\) passes through a normal shock. Determine \(M_2\), \(p_2\), \(T_2\), \(\rho_2/\rho_1\), \(p_{0,2}/p_{0,1}\), and the pressure a Pitot probe would measure.

1
\[M_2=0.57735.\]
2
\[\frac{p_2}{p_1}=4.5,\qquad p_2=225.0\ \text{kPa}.\]
3
\[\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=2.66667,\qquad \frac{T_2}{T_1}=1.6875,\qquad T_2=371.25\ \text{K}.\]
4
\[\frac{p_{0,2}}{p_{0,1}}=0.720874.\]
5
\[\frac{p_{0,2}}{p_1}=5.64044,\qquad p_{\mathrm{Pitot}}=p_{0,2}=282.0\ \text{kPa}.\]

Checks

Common mistakes

  • Reporting the downstream static pressure \(p_2\) as the Pitot reading.
  • Assuming that \(p_{0,2}=p_{0,1}\) because the shock is adiabatic.
  • Using \(M_1\) in the post-shock isentropic relation instead of \(M_2\).
  • Applying normal-shock equations when \(M_1\leq1\).
  • Confusing the rise in static pressure with an increase in available mechanical energy.

Objetivos de aprendizaje

Lo que debes ser capaz de hacer

  • Aplicar conservación de masa, cantidad de movimiento y energía a una onda normal.
  • Calcular \(M_2\) y las razones de presión, densidad, temperatura y presión total.
  • Explicar por qué una onda normal estacionaria física requiere \(M_1>1\).
  • Distinguir el aumento de presión estática de la pérdida de presión total.
  • Interpretar correctamente una medición Pitot subsónica o supersónica.

Comportamiento físico

Una compresión abrupta e irreversible

Una onda normal es una región extremadamente delgada en la que un flujo supersónico se vuelve subsónico. Aumentan la presión estática, densidad, temperatura y entropía; disminuyen la velocidad y el número de Mach.

Se conservan: flujo másico, entalpía total y temperatura total para una onda adiabática sin trabajo.
No se conservan: la entropía aumenta y la presión total disminuye.

Volumen de control

Tres ecuaciones de conservación

\[\boxed{\rho_1u_1=\rho_2u_2}\]
\[\boxed{p_1+\rho_1u_1^2=p_2+\rho_2u_2^2}\]
\[\boxed{h_1+\frac{u_1^2}{2}=h_2+\frac{u_2^2}{2}}\]
¿Por qué la onda es irreversible si las regiones externas se tratan como inviscidas?

Dentro del espesor microscópico de la onda existen gradientes muy intensos. La viscosidad y la conducción térmica generan entropía. El modelo de salto relaciona los estados uniformes sin resolver la estructura interna.

Solución cerrada

Relaciones de choque normal

\[\boxed{M_2^2=\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2-1)}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2}}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{p_2/p_1}{\rho_2/\rho_1}}\]
Para aire con \(\gamma=1.4\), la razón máxima de densidades del modelo es \(6\).

Segunda ley

Entropía y pérdida de presión total

\[ \boxed{ \frac{s_2-s_1}{R} = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) } \]
\[ \boxed{ \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_{0,1}} = \left[\frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2}\right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} \left[\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma M_1^2-(\gamma-1)}\right]^{1/(\gamma-1)} } \]
La presión estática aumenta a través del choque, pero la presión total recuperable disminuye.

Instrumentación

Mediciones Pitot subsónicas y supersónicas

Flujo subsónico

\[ p_{\mathrm{Pitot}}=p_{0,1}, \qquad M_1^2= \frac{2}{\gamma-1} \left[ \left(\frac{p_{0,1}}{p_1}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}-1 \right]. \]

Flujo supersónico

La línea central cruza una porción localmente normal de la onda de proa y después se desacelera isoentrópicamente. La sonda mide \(p_{0,2}\), no \(p_2\) ni \(p_{0,1}\).

\[ \boxed{ \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_1} = \left[ \frac{(\gamma+1)^2M_1^2}{4\gamma M_1^2-2(\gamma-1)} \right]^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)} \left[ \frac{1-\gamma+2\gamma M_1^2}{\gamma+1} \right] } \]
Para aire, \(p_{\mathrm{Pitot}}/p_1=1.8929\) separa las ramas subsónica y supersónica.

Verificaciones

Errores comunes

  • Reportar \(p_2\) como la lectura del Pitot.
  • Suponer que \(p_{0,2}=p_{0,1}\) porque la onda es adiabática.
  • Usar \(M_1\) en la desaceleración isoentrópica posterior al choque.
  • Aplicar relaciones de choque normal cuando \(M_1\leq1\).

Lernziele

Normalstoßwellen und Pitot-Messung

Diese deutsche Kurzfassung enthält die wichtigsten Gleichungen. Die vollständige Herleitung und Beispiele stehen derzeit auf Englisch und Spanisch zur Verfügung.

\[\boxed{M_2^2=\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2-1)}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2}}\]
Bei einer adiabatischen Normalstoßwelle bleibt die Stautemperatur konstant, der Totaldruck sinkt jedoch infolge der Entropieerzeugung.

Überschall-Pitot-Sonde

Vor der Sonde entsteht eine Bugstoßwelle. Gemessen wird der Totaldruck hinter der Stoßwelle \(p_{0,2}\), nicht der statische Druck \(p_2\).
